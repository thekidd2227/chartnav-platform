# ChartNav Company Deck

> General-purpose company overview. 10 slides. Two clearly marked
> credibility tracks inside the same deck so the operator can
> route the right framing to the right audience without
> confusing private-practice buyers with federal credentials.

**Audience:** mixed — advisors, partners, recruiting, federal /
government-healthcare conversations, private-practice owners
seeing the company narrative.
**Purpose:** explain who is behind ChartNav and why the workflow
exists, with audience-appropriate credibility framing.
**CTA / next step:** schedule a fake-patient demo OR open the
pilot conversation.

**Safe-claims contract.** Every slide obeys the approved-language
list at `docs/commercial/chartnav-approved-claims-language.md`.
No HIPAA / SOC 2 / FDA / certified-EHR / autonomous-diagnosis /
order / coding / referral / patient-messaging claims.

**Audience routing.** Slide 8 is the **federal / government-
healthcare credibility slide.** It is appropriate for federal-
healthcare-adjacent buyers, agency partners with federal
relationships, and investors. **Skip slide 8** when the audience
is a private-practice ophthalmology buyer — they care about the
clinical workflow, not federal contracting credentials.

## Slide 3 — Why ChartNav

- **Title:** Why we built this.
- **Content:**
  - Ophthalmology charting drifts; OD/OS workflow is paper-or-
    one-off; pre-visit prep is manual.
  - Existing AI scribes are specialty-agnostic.
  - We chose to build provider-reviewed and ophthalmology-
    specific by construction.
- **Speaker notes:** This is the moat.
- **Visual:** three-bullet card.

## Slide 4 — Clinical Signal Filtering (the prime feature)

- **Title:** Filters conversation. Captures findings. Builds the diagram.
- **Content:**
  - Doctors do not dictate in perfect templates.
  - ChartNav separates casual speech from clinical findings,
    flags uncertainty, and proposes retinal diagram annotations.
  - The provider applies, edits, or rejects every proposal
    before anything is saved or finalized.
- **Speaker notes:** Pause on "the provider applies, edits, or
  rejects" — this is what every audience needs to internalize.
- **Visual:** four-row card showing the four classifications
  (ignored chatter / clinical finding / uncertain phrase /
  proposed annotation).

## Slide 6 — Provider-in-control principles

- **Title:** Our safety principles.
- **Content:**
  - Draft → review → finalize.
  - Signed retinal artifacts are immutable; edits create an
    explicit fork.
  - Audit-friendly design with metadata-only logging.
  - Per-organization isolation; cross-organization requests
    fail closed by design.
  - **No orders. No coding. No referrals. No patient messages.**
- **Speaker notes:** Read these aloud.
- **Visual:** state diagram + 5-bullet panel.

## Slide 7 — Product modules (one line each)

- **Title:** What each module does.
- **Content:**
  - **Clinical Signal Filtering** — separates chatter, findings,
    uncertainty, and proposes diagram annotations.
  - **AI scribe session lifecycle** — draft → review → finalize
    with provider clicks at each transition.
  - **Findings-to-retinal-diagram proposal review** — proposes
    OD/OS annotations the provider applies, edits, or rejects.
  - **OD/OS retinal drawing canvas** — first-class diagram with
    closed symbol vocabulary; signed = immutable; edits fork.
  - **Patient-friendly summary draft** — provider-reviewed
    summary derived from finalized scribe text.
  - **Pre-visit clinical brief** — derived view of available
    chart records with explicit data gaps.
  - **Provider action review queue** — review tasks only;
    suggested → accepted → completed.
  - **Guided demo mode** — opt-in presenter overlay for
    onboarding new providers.
- **Speaker notes:** This is the depth slide. Match talking
  time to the audience's interest.
- **Visual:** 8-card grid with one line each.
