# Phase 24D — Pilot Outreach Message Bank

> **Phase:** 24D — Pilot Practice Selection & Outreach Packaging.
> **Audience:** sales / founder writing the first wave of outreach
> to ophthalmology practices.
> **Companion docs:**
> `phase-24d-pilot-practice-selection-criteria.md` — who to target.
> `phase-24d-pilot-discovery-call-script.md` — what to say when
> they reply.
> `phase-24d-pilot-objection-cheat-sheet.md` — buyer-safe answers.

## How to use this document

Pick the variant that matches the channel (email, LinkedIn) and the
relationship (warm intro vs. cold). Personalize the **bracketed
fields** before sending — never send a template untouched.

Tone rules (apply to every variant below):

- Direct. Short paragraphs. No buzzwords.
- Ophthalmology-specific. Lead with the lane cycle, not "AI."
- Workflow-layer positioning. Not "scribe." Not "EHR."
- Fake-data demo first. Mention it explicitly.
- No overclaims. The approved-phrase list (§"Approved phrases"
  below) is the floor; the forbidden-phrase list (§"Forbidden
  phrases") is the wall.

## Approved phrases

Use these verbatim when you need them. They are the words the
product can actually defend.

- "fake-data retina workflow demo"
- "ophthalmology clinic workflow layer"
- "provider-reviewed documentation"
- "imaging metadata review"
- "role-based clinic dashboards"
- "internal coordination"
- "controlled pilot readiness"
- "Morgan Lee fake-data retina follow-up"
- "front desk → technician → MD → reviewer → internal follow-up"

## Forbidden phrases

Do not use any of these in outreach. They will rot the safe-claims
contract.

- "HIPAA compliant" / "HIPAA certified"
- "certified EHR" / "replaces your EHR" / "EHR replacement"
- "autonomous diagnosis"
- "automatic OCT interpretation" / "OCT interpretation"
- "auto-grade DR" / "auto-select IOL" / "auto-recommend anti-VEGF"
- "automatic orders" / "automatic referrals" / "automatic patient
  messaging"
- "automatic coding" / "automatic billing" / "claims submission"
- "chart fills itself" / "note writes itself"
- "hands-free scribing" (as a primary claim)
- "powered by IBM" / "powered by watsonx"
- a specific OCT or fundus-camera vendor by name

If any of these slips into a draft, rewrite the draft. Do not patch
around it.

**Subject:** Quick follow-up on [Mutual Contact]'s intro — retina
workflow demo

Hi [First Name],

the workflow coordination side of ophthalmology — front desk
readiness, technician workup, imaging metadata review, and the
internal follow-up tasking that tends to get lost between visits.

I'm building ChartNav, an **ophthalmology clinic workflow layer**.
It is not a generic AI scribe; it coordinates the lane cycle and
surfaces role-based clinic dashboards (front desk, technician, MD,
reviewer, admin), with **provider-reviewed documentation** and
**imaging metadata review** in one workspace. Everything runs on
fake data first — no real PHI involved.

Would you be open to a **30-minute fake-data retina workflow demo**
that walks one patient (Morgan Lee, fully synthetic) from front
desk readiness through provider sign-off and internal follow-up
tasking? You'll see exactly what the product does and does not do
— no autonomous diagnosis, no automatic orders, no automatic
billing or claims, no patient messaging.

Happy to send three time slots that work for your team.

Thanks,
[Your Name]
[Your Title], ChartNav
[Your contact info]

touches real PHI.

## Variant 3 — Cold email to retina practice / retina physician

**Subject:** Retina follow-up coordination at [Practice Name]

Hi [Dr. Last Name],

I'm building **ChartNav**, an ophthalmology clinic workflow layer
with a specific wedge for retina follow-ups. The product
coordinates the lane cycle around a retina visit — front desk
readiness, technician workup, OCT macula + fundus photo metadata
review, retina tracking, provider-reviewed documentation,
sign-off, and the internal follow-up tasking that closes the loop
with the front desk.

It is **not** a diagnostic tool, an image-interpretation tool, or
an EHR. It does not auto-grade DR, recommend anti-VEGF dosing, or
select IOLs. Those decisions stay with the retina team.

What it does do: makes the lane cycle visible across roles so a
follow-up does not get dropped because the OCT did not surface to
the right person at the right time, and so the next-visit window
gets confirmed by internal staff (not by a patient-facing message
the practice did not authorize).

I'd be glad to show you a **fake-data retina workflow demo** — 30
minutes, no real PHI, one synthetic patient end-to-end. You'll
see the safe-claims contract live in the product, not just in the
brochure.

Three slots: [Day, Time 1], [Day, Time 2], [Day, Time 3].

Thanks,
[Your Name]
ChartNav
